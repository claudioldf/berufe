# Berufe — Recurrence Strategy: MVP Gap Review

## 1. What this document is

`Berufe_Product_Strategy_Recurrence_Notes.md` argues that Berufe should be the tool a professional uses to run their jobs, and that the portfolio, reputation, and network should fall out of that use. This document checks that argument against the code as it exists today and lists **only what is still missing from the strategy's "Recommended MVP → Initial version" list**.

Deliberately out of scope here: the strategy doc's "Second version" and "After validation" lists, and its growth-engine and partnership sections. They are captured in the source document and should stay there until the MVP loop is closed.

This is a **review, not approved scope**. `Berufe_MVP_Stories.md` and the increment plans remain the source of truth for what is being built. Several gaps below are currently deferred to `Berufe_V2_Stories.md` or explicitly excluded — section 4 marks those, because closing them requires a product decision, not just an implementation ticket.

The pattern is clear: **the identity and network layers are built; the operation layer is not.** Everything Berufe knows today is what a professional _declares_ about themselves plus what another professional _vouches for_. Nothing is generated by a job actually being done.

## 3. What is missing

### 3.1 The client cannot act on a quote

The strategy expects the client to open the link and approve, request a change, decline, contact the professional, or accept the terms. Today the link is read-only: `Quote::STATUSES` is `%w[draft shared]` (`apps/api/app/models/quote.rb:4`, enforced by the `quotes_known_status` check constraint), and the shared page tells the client outright that the link "não representa aceite, assinatura ou pagamento" (`apps/web/app/pages/orcamento/[token].vue:94`).

Missing:

- Client-facing decision states on the quote — at minimum `approved`, `declined`, and `change_requested` — with the timestamp and, for a change request, the client's message.
- An anonymous, token-authenticated decision endpoint alongside the existing `POST /shared-quotes/resolve`, with the same generic not-found behavior for invalid, revoked, or unpublished-owner tokens.
- Idempotency and immutability rules: what happens when the professional edits a quote that was already approved (today, editing a shared quote silently preserves the token and returns the newest content to the client).
- Surfacing the decision back to the professional — the dashboard's recent-quotes list currently shows only `draft`/`shared`.

This is the single highest-leverage gap. Without it there is no approval event, and without an approval event there is no job, no completion, no evidence, and no reason to come back.

### 3.2 The quote does not carry the job's commercial terms

`ProfessionalQuoteWriteRequest` accepts `customer_name`, `service_description`, `discount_amount`, `valid_until`, `notes`, and up to 20 generic line items (`description`, `quantity`, `unit`, `unit_price`).

Missing against the strategy's field list:

- **Materials vs. labor.** Line items are untyped, so the quote cannot state the split the strategy calls for, and no metric can be derived from it later.
- **Timeline.** `valid_until` is the validity of the offer, not the duration or start date of the work.
- **Payment method and terms.** Only expressible informally inside the 700-character `notes` field.
- **Service address.** Not captured — yet "look up the address and date of the service" is one of the strategy's core reasons to log in.
- **Photos or attachments on the quote.** The media-upload pipeline exists (`media_uploads`, `MediaUploadReceiver`, `MediaUploadProcessor`) but has no `purpose` for quotes.
- **Catalog linkage.** `service_description` is free text, so a quote cannot be attributed to a catalog service, and therefore cannot feed "categories actually performed."

### 3.3 There is no service (job) entity

This is the structural gap. The strategy's whole loop — scheduled date, status, approved scope, changes, amounts received and outstanding, professionals involved, completion — assumes an object that exists after approval and before reputation. No such table, model, endpoint, or page exists.

Everything else in this section depends on it. Introducing it means deciding:

- Whether a job is a state of `quotes` or a separate record created on approval (a separate record is the better fit: scope can change, and the quote should stay the immutable record of what was agreed).
- Which of the strategy's fields are MVP (scheduled date, status, approved scope, completion confirmation) and which are not (received/outstanding amounts touch payments, which are excluded today).
- How scope changes are recorded — an append-only change log preserves the "protects both parties against misunderstandings" property the strategy is actually buying.

### 3.4 Completion confirmation

Missing entirely. This is the event the strategy uses as the trust anchor: it is what makes "18 confirmed services" mean more than a self-declared profile. It needs a client-side confirmation on the same tokenized surface as the quote decision, and a professional-side request-for-confirmation action.

### 3.5 Photos are portfolio uploads, not job evidence

`portfolio_items` are moderated, published per item, and reference a catalog `service_id` — not a job, and not a client. Missing:

- A link from a photo to the job it came from.
- Before/after pairing. Already logged as an evidence-triggered V2 candidate ("Before/after pairs and project albums"), but the strategy makes it MVP because the pair _is_ the proof.
- Client authorization at publication time. The strategy has the professional choose which photos may be published **after** the client confirms completion; today publication is professional-declared and admin-moderated, with the client never consulted.
- Automatic portfolio maintenance. Today portfolio entries are hand-created and hand-managed; the strategy's point is that they should be a by-product.

### 3.6 Verified reviews

No rating, review, or recommendation-from-client concept exists anywhere in the API, the contract, or the web app. The nearest existing thing is `professional_relationships`, which is professional-to-professional (`recommendation`, `worked_together`) — a different trust edge.

Client recommendations already exist as written V2 stories (V2-008 create and share a request, V2-009 phone-confirmed submission, V2-010 moderate and publish). The strategy's version is stronger and cheaper: a review tied to a confirmed job needs no separate confirmation flow, because the client already proved they were the client when they approved the quote and confirmed completion.

### 3.7 The public profile shows no evidence counters

`PublicProfessionalProfileSerializer` publishes services, coverage, verification labels, portfolio, relationships, and social links. It has no place for the strategy's example sentence — "18 confirmed services, 14 client recommendations, 6 verified professionals worked with."

Two of those numbers cannot exist without sections 3.3–3.6. The third (professionals worked with) is derivable **today** from accepted `professional_relationships` and is the one counter that could ship immediately.

Also missing from the strategy's evidence list, and already deferred: CNPJ/MEI and certificate verification. `verification_requests` is constrained to `verification_type = 'identity'` at the database level (`apps/api/db/schema.rb:723`); company and certificate verification is V2-002.

### 3.8 There is no client record

`quotes.customer_name` is an 80-character free-text string. There is no client entity, no client phone, and no way to recognize that two quotes went to the same person. That blocks: the client directory, "repeat client" as an evidence signal, quote history per client, and any re-contact flow.

This one has a privacy dimension that should be settled before it is built — Berufe currently stores almost nothing about non-users, and a client directory changes that. It needs a retention window and a stated basis in the privacy policy, consistent with how `search_events` and verification evidence are already handled.

### 3.9 There is no return path

The strategy's recurrence depends on the professional being pulled back in ("your quote was viewed", "the client approved", "confirm the completion") and landing directly on the relevant screen. Today Berufe never initiates contact — WhatsApp is only an outbound link the professional clicks (`PublicWhatsappUrl`, quote share), and automated WhatsApp messaging is explicitly excluded from the MVP.

Without a decision here, every state change in sections 3.1–3.4 is invisible until the professional happens to open the dashboard, which is the exact behavior the strategy is trying to fix. The cheapest MVP-compatible answer is not automated messaging: it is making the state changes visible and actionable on the dashboard, and letting the _client's_ approval notification be the professional's WhatsApp message — the client already has the professional's number.

### 3.10 No PDF

The strategy asks for "a link and a professional PDF." Only the link exists; the shared page offers `window.print()` as the fallback (`apps/web/app/pages/orcamento/[token].vue`). Server-side PDF generation is explicitly listed as out of MVP scope.

## 4. Gaps that need a scope decision, not just a ticket

| Gap                                  | Current position                                                       |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Quote acceptance (3.1)               | V2 candidate — "Quote PDF, acceptance, templates, and version history" |
| Quote PDF (3.10)                     | Explicitly excluded — "server PDF generation"                          |
| Service/job entity, schedule (3.3)   | Explicitly excluded — "booking"                                        |
| Amounts received/outstanding (3.3)   | Explicitly excluded — "payment systems"                                |
| Before/after pairs (3.5)             | Evidence-triggered V2 candidate                                        |
| Client reviews (3.6)                 | V2-008, V2-009, V2-010                                                 |
| CNPJ/MEI and certificates (3.7)      | V2-002                                                                 |
| Client directory (3.8)               | Explicitly excluded — "a generic CRM"                                  |
| Berufe-initiated notifications (3.9) | Explicitly excluded — "automated WhatsApp messaging"                   |

The strategy document's central claim is that this list is inverted: these are not features to add once the MVP proves itself, they are the reason a professional would use the MVP at all. Resolving that disagreement is the decision to make before any of the tickets below are written.

## 5. The smallest slice that closes the loop

If the strategy is accepted, this is the shortest path from what exists today to "every use of the system strengthens the operation, the reputation, and the network," in dependency order:

1. **Client decision on a shared quote** (3.1) — approve, decline, request a change. Reuses the existing token, resolver, and page. No new entity.
2. **A job created on approval** (3.3), minimally: approved scope snapshot, scheduled date, address, status.
3. **Completion confirmation by the client** (3.4) on the same tokenized surface.
4. **Job photos with client-authorized publication** (3.5), reusing `media_uploads` and the existing moderation queue.
5. **A review tied to a confirmed job** (3.6).
6. **Evidence counters on the public profile** (3.7), fed by 2–5, plus the "professionals worked with" count that is already derivable.

Steps 1 and 6's relationship counter are additive to what exists and could be built without any new domain concept. Step 2 is the commitment point — everything after it depends on Berufe holding a record of work, not just a record of people.

Quote field completeness (3.2), the client directory (3.8), the return path (3.9), and the PDF (3.10) are real gaps but none of them are on the critical path of the loop. They make the loop pleasant; steps 1–6 make it exist.
